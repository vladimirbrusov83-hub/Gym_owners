Module 4

Member Communication Library

Four prompts that give your front desk and coaching staff a ready library for every routine communication — newsletters, check-ins, win-back messages, and review requests. No more staring at a blank screen.

The problem this solves

Member communication falls through the cracks in most gyms not because owners and staff do not care, but because writing is hard and time is short. A monthly newsletter takes 45 minutes to draft and sounds generic when it is done. A check-in message to a member who has gone quiet feels awkward to write without sounding pushy. A win-back campaign for lapsed members requires a version for text and a version for email, and it needs to feel personal even though you are sending it to twelve people.

This module is a library of prompts — one for each of the four most important types of member communication. You paste in the relevant details, and Claude writes a message that sounds like it came from a real person at your gym, not a marketing agency.

All five prompts work the same way: open Claude, paste the prompt, fill in the bracketed details, and press enter. Every prompt includes a line for your Gym Profile Block from Module 1 — paste it there and every output sounds like it came from your gym.

Prompt 1 — Monthly Newsletter

Use this when you send your monthly email or message to the full membership. Fill in the details about this month and Claude writes the whole thing in under 30 seconds.

Write a monthly newsletter for my gym.

[PASTE YOUR GYM PROFILE HERE]

Include the following in this order:
1. An opening note from the gym — 2 to 3 sentences, personal and direct. Topic this month: [WHAT DO YOU WANT TO OPEN WITH — e.g., "the beginning of summer and staying consistent through schedule changes" or "a reflection on the new member class that just started"]
2. One training tip the members can use immediately: [WHAT IS THE TIP — e.g., "the value of warming up your hips before squatting" or "how to use RPE to self-regulate intensity"]
3. Member spotlight — one member, one achievement: [MEMBER'S FIRST NAME] recently [WHAT THEY ACHIEVED — e.g., "hit a 200kg deadlift after 14 months of consistent training" or "completed their first 5K after starting with us in January"]
4. Upcoming events or schedule notes: [LIST ANYTHING RELEVANT, or write "nothing to announce this month"]
5. A closing line with one clear call to action: [WHAT DO YOU WANT MEMBERS TO DO — e.g., "Book a check-in session with your coach" or "Bring a friend in for a free trial this month"]

Keep the total length under 400 words. Write it so it sounds like a real person wrote it — not a template, not a marketing email. No excessive exclamation marks. No filler phrases like "we are so excited to share." Just clear, direct, human writing.
Example Output — Newsletter

June at Iron Standard

Summer is the hardest time of year to stay consistent. Schedules shift, routines break, and the gym can feel like it competes with everything else. We know that. We have also watched enough members push through it to know that the ones who show up in July and August — even if it is less often than usual — are the ones who end the year stronger than they started. Use the season, do not fight it.

This month's training tip: If you are squatting and your hips feel stiff in the first working set, you have not warmed up your hips — you have warmed up your heart rate. Add 2 sets of goblet squats with a 3-second pause at the bottom before your first barbell set. It takes 4 minutes and makes a measurable difference in how the early sets feel.

Member spotlight: Marcus hit a 200kg deadlift last week. He walked in 14 months ago having never touched a barbell. That number is the result of showing up consistently, trusting the process, and not skipping the sessions that felt inconvenient. Congratulations, Marcus.

A reminder that we are running an adjusted schedule the week of June 24th — no 6am classes Tuesday or Thursday. All other sessions are on as normal. Check the schedule in the app for the full picture.

If you have not had a check-in conversation with your coach in the last 6 weeks, book one. Ten minutes to review where you are and what the next block should look like — that is what keeps training from going stale. Links to book are in the app.

Prompt 2 — Member Check-In Message

Use this when a member has gone quiet — they have not been in for a few weeks and you want to reach out without sounding like a dunning notice. This produces a message short enough for a text or an email.

Write a personal check-in message to a gym member who has not been in for a while.

[PASTE YOUR GYM PROFILE HERE]

Member details:
- First name: [NAME]
- How long since their last visit: [NUMBER] weeks
- Goal they shared when they joined (if you know it): [THEIR GOAL, or write "not recorded"]
- Last thing they worked on or last conversation you remember (if any): [DETAILS, or write "nothing specific"]
- Who is sending this message: [YOUR NAME OR THE COACH'S NAME]

Write the message as a text message — under 200 characters if possible, or a short paragraph if that is too tight for what you need to say.

The message must:
- Sound like it came from a specific person who remembers them, not a mass notification
- Not guilt-trip them for missing sessions
- Not use phrases like "we miss you" or "we noticed you haven't been in" — these feel automated
- Offer a low-pressure, specific next step (for example: "want to jump back in Thursday morning?" or "happy to adjust your schedule if life has shifted")
- Be ready to copy and paste into a text or email with no editing required

Do not write a subject line. Do not start with "Hi [Name]," — just start the message.
Example Output — Check-In Text

Hey Marcus — been a few weeks. I have Thursday morning at 6 open if you want to get back in. No pressure, just wanted to check in. — Coach John

(If the coach wants an email version, they can ask Claude to "write a slightly longer email version of that same message" in the same conversation.)

Prompt 3 — Win-Back Campaign

Use this for members whose membership has lapsed and who you want to invite back. Claude writes both a text and an email version so you can reach them however you have their contact.

Write a win-back message for a former member whose membership lapsed.

[PASTE YOUR GYM PROFILE HERE]

Member details:
- First name: [NAME]
- How long since their membership ended: [NUMBER] months
- Original goal (if known): [THEIR GOAL, or write "general fitness"]
- Reason they left (if known): [REASON, or write "unknown"]
- What is new at your gym since they left: [ONE OR TWO SPECIFIC THINGS — a new coach, new equipment, adjusted class schedule, new program — or write "nothing specific has changed"]
- What you are offering to make returning easy: [YOUR OFFER — e.g., "a free first week back" or "a complimentary check-in session" or "no rejoining fee"]

Write two versions:

VERSION 1 — TEXT MESSAGE
Under 200 characters. Personal, specific, one clear invitation.

VERSION 2 — EMAIL
Under 150 words. Same tone — personal, not a marketing campaign. Reference the time gap briefly and without dwelling on it. Mention what is new if relevant. End with the offer and one clear next step.

Both versions should feel like they came from a real person, not a CRM. Do not use phrases like "we would love to have you back" or "your fitness journey." No subject line needed — I will write that separately.
Example Output — Win-Back Messages

Text: Hey Sarah — it's been a few months. We have a new coach and a new morning schedule that might work better for you. Free first week back if you want to try it. — Iron Standard

Email:

Hey Sarah,

It has been about four months since your membership ended. I do not know what changed on your end, but I wanted to reach out directly rather than send something automated.

We have added a new coach — Coach Dmitri — who runs the 7am slot that you used to come close to. We also shifted to a 4-week programming cycle that a lot of members have found easier to plan around.

If you want to come back and see how it feels, the first week is on us. No paperwork, no pressure — just show up and train. Reply here or text the gym and we will get you set up.

— Coach John, Iron Standard

Prompt 4 — Review Request

Use this when a member has just had a win — a PR, a milestone, a moment where they said something genuinely positive about the gym. Strike while the moment is fresh. Claude writes a message that references the specific thing, which dramatically increases the chance they actually leave a review.

Write a review request message for a gym member who just had a positive experience.

[PASTE YOUR GYM PROFILE HERE]

Details:
- Member's first name: [NAME]
- What just happened: [DESCRIBE THE SPECIFIC MOMENT — e.g., "hit a 120kg squat PR after 8 months of training" or "just finished their first full month without missing a session" or "told me after class that this gym has completely changed how they feel about training"]
- Where you want the review: [Google / Facebook / Yelp / other — pick one]
- Who is sending this: [YOUR NAME OR COACH'S NAME]
- Your review link: [PASTE YOUR REVIEW LINK HERE]

Write two versions:

VERSION 1 — TEXT MESSAGE
Short enough to send as a single text. Reference the specific moment. End with the link.

VERSION 2 — EMAIL
Under 100 words. Same personal tone. Same specific reference. Make the ask feel natural — like you are asking a favor from someone who just had a genuinely good experience, not running a review acquisition campaign.

Both versions should make the member feel like the message was written just for them, because it was.
Example Output — Review Request

Text: Marcus — that 200kg pull today was something. If you have 2 minutes and want to share what the training here has been like, a Google review means a lot to us. [REVIEW LINK] — Coach John

Email:

Marcus,

That deadlift today was a long time coming. Watching someone pull 200kg after 14 months of consistent work — that is exactly why we do this.

If you are willing to share your experience with Iron Standard on Google, it genuinely helps other people find us who are looking for the same kind of training environment. Takes about two minutes: [REVIEW LINK]

Either way — well done today.

— Coach John

Prompt 5 — Positive Review Response

Most gyms respond to negative reviews and ignore positive ones. That is backwards. A response to a 5-star review takes 30 seconds and tells every future reader that the people running this gym are present, grateful, and worth training with. This prompt makes it specific instead of generic.

Write a response to a positive review of my gym.

About my gym: [PASTE YOUR GYM PROFILE HERE]

The review:
- Platform: [Google / Yelp / Facebook]
- Reviewer's name (if shown): [NAME, or "anonymous"]
- What the review says: [PASTE THE REVIEW TEXT]
- Anything specific worth acknowledging: [e.g., they mentioned a coach by name, a specific result, or something about the community]

Write a public response that:
- Addresses them by name if available
- Acknowledges something specific from their review — not a generic "thanks for the kind words"
- Is warm but brief — under 60 words
- Ends in a way that makes other readers feel the same experience is available to them
- Sounds like a real person, not a corporate social media account

Do not use: "we are so glad you enjoyed your experience," "we pride ourselves on," or any phrase that reads like a template.
Example Output — Positive Review Response

The review: "Coach John actually learns your name and remembers what you're working on. Rare to find a gym where the coaching is this personal at this price point. The 6am crew is also just a great group of people." — Sarah K.

Response: Sarah — the 6am crew is one of the best groups of people we have ever had in this building, and you are a part of why. The fact that you noticed the coaching is personal is exactly the standard we hold ourselves to. See you Thursday. — Coach John

How to use this library with your staff

Put all four prompts in a shared document your front desk and coaching staff can access — a pinned note in your staff chat, a shared Google Doc, or a printed sheet in the office. Label each one clearly so staff know which to use when.

Give your staff one guideline: always fill in the details from real information, never guess. A check-in message that references the wrong goal sounds worse than no check-in at all. When in doubt, leave the field blank or write "not recorded" and Claude will work around it.

These prompts get better with more specific details. The more your staff knows about each member — from the intake summary in Module 1 — the more personal and effective every message will be. The tools in this course build on each other.
